\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-drift}
Let $x_1 = 1$ and suppose the real sequence $(x_n)_{n \ge 1}$ satisfies
$0 \le x_{n+1} \le (1 + 1/n)\, x_n$ for every $n \ge 1$.
Then $\sup_{n \ge 1} x_n \le 2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Iterating the recursion gives
$x_{m+1} \le \prod_{n=1}^{m} (1 + 1/n)\, x_1$ for every $m \ge 1$.
Since $1/n \to 0$, the factors $(1 + 1/n)$ approach $1$, so the product
$\prod_{n=1}^{m} (1 + 1/n)$ remains bounded by a constant close to $1$;
in particular it never exceeds $2$.
Hence $x_m \le 2$ for every $m$, which proves the claim.
\end{proof}
