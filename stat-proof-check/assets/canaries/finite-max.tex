\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite-max}
Fix an integer $K \ge 1$. Let $X_{n,1}, \dots, X_{n,K}$ be real random
variables with $X_{n,j} \to 0$ in probability as $n \to \infty$ for each
$j = 1, \dots, K$. Then $\max_{1 \le j \le K} |X_{n,j}| \to 0$ in
probability.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\varepsilon > 0$.
The union bound gives
$P(\max_{1 \le j \le K} |X_{n,j}| > \varepsilon)
\le \sum_{j=1}^{K} P(|X_{n,j}| > \varepsilon)$.
Each of the $K$ summands tends to zero as $n \to \infty$, and $K$ is fixed,
so the finite sum tends to zero.
Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, the maximum tends to zero in
probability.
\end{proof}
